Screening approach adapted for retail banking

  • Study Frame: Madurai Corporation urban frame divided into 1,248 equal hexagonal blocks (H3 resolution 9, ~10.5 hectares each). 1,114 blocks with residential/commercial population are evaluated.
  • Step 1, Format Fit (Commercial Match): Each block is ranked on six commercial vibrancy and business demand factors against all other blocks in Madurai; the factors carry equal weight (1 of 6 each).
  • Step 2, Headroom (Room for a New Branch): Each block is ranked on six market gap and growth metrics, weighted 30%, 25%, 20%, 10%, 10%, and 5%.
  • Thresholds: A block qualifies as Best Opportunity if its commercial match is in the top 25% of the city (fit ≥ 75) and its unserved banking headroom is in the top 30% (headroom ≥ 70).

How opportunity areas were formed in Madurai

  1. Spatial Grouping: Contiguous "Best opportunity" blocks were merged into consolidated search zones.
  2. Exclusion of Water Buffers: Blocks bordering the Vaigai river flood corridor and irrigation tanks (Vandiyur, Teppakulam perimeter buffers) were excluded from commercial centroid formation.
  3. Network Proximity Checks: Blocks within a 2.5 km road radius of your bank's active branches were reviewed to ensure zero cannibalization.
  4. Ranking: Opportunity areas were sorted by their composite 1-to-5 suitability rating:
    Rating = 1 + 4 × (0.5 × Fit Percentile + 0.5 × Headroom Percentile) / 100

Key evaluative takeaways

  • Commercial Gravity: Site 2 (Kamarajar Salai) sits on Madurai's premier commercial spine connecting Munichalai to Teppakulam, capturing intensive merchant cash deposits, textile/gold businesses, and high daily business footfall.
  • Retail vs Commercial Trade: Site 1 (Bethaniyapuram) acts as a residential bedroom suburb along the Dindigul highway corridor.   • Deposit Depth: Site 2's 2 km radius houses over 320,000 residents and more than 4,500 trading enterprises, generating over 2.4x the CASA deposit velocity of Site 1.

Business mix distinction between candidate corridors

  • Commercial & Merchant Model (Site 2): High float of low-cost Current Accounts (CASA ratio > 46%) driven by daily cash credits from the surrounding wholesale textile, gold, and grocery merchants. High velocity of high-margin Gold Loans and merchant overdrafts.
  • Consumer Retail Model (Site 1): Dominated by Savings Bank (SB) deposits from salaried families and small retirees. Loan portfolio is asset-backed consumer debt (home loans, two-wheeler/four-wheeler vehicle loans) with longer payback cycles (26–30 months).

Data integrity and audit standards

  • Zero Scraping Policy: Commercial rents and popular hours are collected through authorized APIs or manual surveys into structured templates.
  • Mid-Rank Percentile Rule: All scores are standardized against other blocks within Madurai Corporation to avoid cross-city distortion.
  • Single Source of Truth: The quantitative engine guarantees that figures displayed in summary tables, scorecards, and charts match without discrepancies.
